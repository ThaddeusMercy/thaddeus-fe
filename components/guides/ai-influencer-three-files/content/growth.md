# Growing the page

The files make her consistent. They do not make her interesting. Consistency is what keeps an audience once you have one, and the first ninety days are about earning one.

## Post before you sell

The first thirty days, sell nothing. No link in bio, no product, no discount code. An account that starts selling before anyone knows the character reads as a funnel, and people leave funnels.

Thirty days of posting also gives you something the files cannot: a content log with real numbers in it, so you know which of her opinions people argue with and which ones get scrolled past.

## Cadence

- One reel a day, minimum, for the first ninety days
- Three to five story frames daily, this is where the persona does most of the work
- One carousel or static post a week, for the posts people save
- Reply to every comment in the first hour, every single time

Daily posting is not about volume for its own sake. A new account gets tested on a small audience, and one post a day gives you seven tests a week instead of two.

If a daily reel is not possible, three a week posted forever beats seven a week for a month and then nothing.

## The content mix

Roughly, and adjust once you have data:

- Half of it teaches or entertains inside the niche, this is what gets shared
- A quarter is her life, the apartment, the gym, the routine, this is what makes people care
- A quarter is her opinions, this is what gets comments and it is the part people skip

The third bucket is the one that grows the page. An account with no positions gets watched and forgotten.

## What actually moves

- Series formats. Numbered, repeatable, the same hook each time. People follow to see the next one.
- One hook you can run twenty times with different content.
- Trending audio, used the week it trends, not the month after.
- Collaborations with real accounts in the niche, because their audience trusts them and that trust carries.
- Replying in the comments as her, in her typing style, with the memory file open.

## What does not

- Buying followers. It breaks the ratio, kills your reach, and no brand pays for a dead audience.
- Posting at "the best time" instead of the same time. Consistency beats timing.
- Chasing every trend outside the niche for a spike in views from people who will never come back.
- Long captions on a reel nobody finished watching.

## Metrics worth reading

- Saves and shares, over likes, because both signal the post was worth keeping or sending
- Profile visits from a post, which tells you the hook worked
- Follows from a post, which tells you the character worked
- Reply rate in DMs, which is the number brands care about and almost nobody tracks

Log the good ones in brain.md. A hook that worked in month two is a hook you can run again in month six with different content, and you will not remember it otherwise.

## When to monetize

Start when two of these are true: the account has passed ten thousand followers, a post has gone past a hundred thousand views, or people are already asking her where to buy something.

Brand deals come after digital products, not before. Having something of your own selling gives you a number to negotiate against, and it means you are not dependent on a brand saying yes.

## Say she is AI

Label the account. Put it in the bio, keep the exact answer from persona.md ready for when someone asks in the DMs, and use the platform's AI label on posts where it applies.

People are fine with an AI character. People are not fine with finding out they were fooled, and one screenshot of you denying it undoes a year of posting. The accounts that got in trouble are the ones that pretended.
